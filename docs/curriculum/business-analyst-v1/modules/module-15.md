## The Engagement

The student receives an **Executive Brief**: a company profile, financials, the stakeholder landscape, the
presenting problems, constraints, AI opportunities, and regulatory concerns — ambiguous and conflicted, the
way a real engagement arrives. From there they run a complete discovery-to-transformation engagement across
five phases, each producing real deliverables and integrating the modules that taught them. The engagement
is graded on the artifacts *and* the boardroom defense; pass ≥80, distinction ≥92.

---

## Phase 1 · Discovery
**Integrates:** M3 (stakeholders) · M4 (elicitation) · M10 (discovery) · M11 (decision intelligence)
**Produces:** Executive Discovery Report (**P1**)

The student separates the loud requests from the evidenced problems: maps stakeholders and politics, runs
elicitation, validates with evidence (behavioral over attitudinal), and frames the **real** problem — not
the one the sponsor walked in with. Output: the **Executive Discovery Report** (problem · opportunities ·
evidence/confidence · recommendation · risks) with a Working-Backwards narrative. *Deliverable answers: what
is actually wrong, and how do we know?*

## Phase 2 · Architecture & Strategy
**Integrates:** M8 (business architecture) · M9 (product strategy)
**Produces:** Capability Map + Target-State Architecture (toward **P8**) · Product Opportunity Assessment (**P4**)

The student lifts to the enterprise: a **capability map** and **capability heatmap** locate where the
strategy is blocked (and where strategic debt drags it), a **target-state architecture** defines where the
business must go, and a **Product Opportunity Assessment** chooses the few opportunities worth pursuing —
each traced to strategy through capability. *Deliverable answers: where must the business go, and which bets
get us there?*

## Phase 3 · Requirements, Governance & Risk
**Integrates:** M6 (requirements) · M7 (process) · M13 (AI) · M14 (compliance)
**Produces:** AI Requirements Package (**P3**) · AI Governance Compliance Blueprint · Risk Treatment Plan

The student makes it real and safe: full-ladder **AI Requirements** for the recommended solution, the
**process** redesign behind it, an **AI Governance Compliance Blueprint** for the regulated/AI dimensions,
and a **Risk Treatment Plan** (accept/mitigate/transfer/avoid against the firm's risk appetite). Every
control answers *"what is the consequence if this fails?"* *Deliverable answers: what do we build, and how do
we keep it trustworthy and compliant?*

## Phase 4 · The Investment Case
**Integrates:** M12 (investment governance)
**Produces:** Business Case (**P5**) + Cost of Delay + Benefits Realization Plan

The student translates the transformation into money: a **Business Case** (TCO · evidenced benefits ·
ROI/payback/NPV), a **Cost of Delay** assessment (what waiting costs), kill-switch criteria, and a **Benefits
Realization Plan** with named owners. This phase exists to answer the **defining question** head-on. *Deliverable
answers: why should we spend money on this — and how will we know it paid off?*

## Phase 5 · The Board Recommendation & Defense
**Integrates:** M3 + M5 (executive communication & facilitation) · everything above
**Produces:** Board Recommendation Deck · **Boardroom Defense (Sim 10)** · the **Aladiah Profile**

The student synthesizes everything into a **Board Recommendation Deck** following the executive narrative —
*Current State → Strategic Risk → Capability Gap → Business Impact → Recommendation → Governance → Investment →
Expected Outcome* — and **defends it live** in **Simulation 10**, where an AI executive board challenges them:

| Board persona | What they attack |
|---|---|
| **CEO** | Is this the right strategic bet? Why now? |
| **CFO** | The numbers — ROI, payback, cost of delay, *why spend the money?* |
| **COO** | Feasibility, operational disruption, the operating-model change |
| **Chief Risk Officer** | Risk exposure, treatment, residual risk, "what if a control fails?" |
| **Head of Product** | Is the opportunity validated? Outcomes over outputs? |
| **Head of Compliance** | Regulatory exposure, AI governance, "what happens if the AI is wrong?" |

Surviving the board means every prior module's discipline holds under pressure. *Deliverable answers: can you
turn ambiguity into a funded, governed, defensible transformation — to a board's face?*
