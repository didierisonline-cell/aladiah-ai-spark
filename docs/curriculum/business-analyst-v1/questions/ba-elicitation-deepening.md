## 2. Question bank (10 new)

**Q1 · JTBD interviews · Practitioner** — correct: index 1
A JTBD interview focuses on:
1. Demographics and segments
2. The customer's timeline and context of struggle when they "hired" a solution
3. Feature wishlists
4. Pricing sensitivity only
**a: 1** — *JTBD interviewing reconstructs the actual timeline/context of the struggle and the progress sought — surfacing the real job, not feature requests.*

**Q2 · Contextual inquiry · Practitioner** — correct: index 3
Contextual inquiry means:
1. Surveying users remotely
2. Running a focus group
3. Reading the documentation
4. Observing and interviewing users in their real environment while they perform tasks
**a: 3** — *Contextual inquiry observes and questions users in their actual environment doing real work — revealing tacit needs and workarounds surveys miss.*

**Q3 · Observation techniques · Foundational** — correct: index 0
Observing what users do is often more reliable than asking because:
1. People's described behavior differs from actual behavior (forgotten steps, workarounds)
2. It's simply faster
3. Observation needs no skill
4. It avoids stakeholders
**a: 0** — *Stated behavior diverges from actual — people omit steps and workarounds. Observation captures the real process and latent needs.*

**Q4 · Ethnographic research · Senior** — correct: index 2
Deep ethnographic research is most warranted when:
1. Requirements are already well understood
2. Time is extremely short
3. The problem space is poorly understood and shaped by culture, context, and unspoken practice
4. A survey already answered everything
**a: 2** — *Ethnographic immersion pays off where the domain is poorly understood and shaped by tacit context/culture — reaching needs other methods can't.*

**Q5 · Root-cause discovery · Practitioner** — correct: index 1
A stakeholder requests "add more reports." The elite elicitation move is:
1. Document the feature as stated
2. Probe for the underlying decision/problem the reports would serve (e.g., 5 Whys)
3. Build it immediately
4. Reject it outright
**a: 1** — *Requests are often solutions in disguise; eliciting the underlying problem/decision reveals the real need and often a better solution.*

**Q6 · Signal detection · Practitioner** — correct: index 3
Across 15 interviews, one vivid story stands out emotionally. The skilled analyst:
1. Treats it as the main requirement
2. Discards all stories
3. Builds for that one user
4. Weighs it against patterns across interviews to separate signal from a memorable anecdote
**a: 3** — *Vivid anecdotes bias judgment; signal detection weighs recurring evidence across sources to tell a true pattern from a one-off.*

**Q7 · Executive interviewing · Practitioner** — correct: index 0
Interviewing a time-constrained executive, the BA should:
1. Prepare focused, high-value questions on strategy, outcomes, constraints, and decision criteria
2. Ask a long list of detailed questions
3. Improvise on the spot
4. Send a survey instead
**a: 0** — *Executive interviews must be tightly prepared and high-leverage — strategy, outcomes, constraints, decision criteria — respecting limited time.*

**Q8 · Difficult stakeholder interviews · Senior** — correct: index 2
A guarded, skeptical stakeholder gives short, unhelpful answers. The best technique is:
1. Push harder with rapid-fire questions
2. Escalate immediately
3. Build rapport and safety, start from their perspective/pain, and use open, story-based questions
4. End the interview
**a: 2** — *With guarded stakeholders, rapport/safety plus open, story-based questions from their world lowers defenses; pressure shuts it down.*

**Q9 · Foundations — stated vs real needs · Foundational** — correct: index 1
The core risk of documenting requirements exactly as stakeholders state them is:
1. Nothing — verbatim is safest
2. Capturing solutions and symptoms rather than the underlying needs
3. Too much detail
4. Slower delivery
**a: 1** — *Verbatim capture often records solutions/symptoms, not needs; elicitation must dig beneath stated requests to the underlying problem and value.*

**Q10 · Observation — go and see · Foundational** — correct: index 3
"Go and see" (genchi genbutsu) in elicitation means:
1. Read reports about the work
2. Ask only managers
3. Rely on system logs
4. Go to where the work happens and observe it directly
**a: 3** — *Going to the actual place of work yields firsthand evidence of the real process and problems — stronger than secondhand reports.*
