# Module 1 — Agile & Scrum Foundations (AI-Augmented)
**Phase:** Foundations · **Tags:** Scrum, Agile, AI · **Reference module (the quality bar).**

## Learning objectives
1. Explain Scrum as a lightweight empirical framework and when to use it.
2. Contrast empirical (Scrum) vs predictive (waterfall) delivery.
3. Apply the three empirical pillars and five Scrum values to real decisions.
4. Use an AI co-pilot to accelerate learning, facilitation, and decision-making.

## Lessons (9) — transcript-first (→ HeyGen video + ElevenLabs audio)
> Each lesson: ~1,200–1,800-word transcript, a downloadable resource, and an assignment. Below are the authored teaching cores (expand each to full transcript for production).

**L1 · What Scrum Is (and Is Not).** Scrum is a framework, not a methodology — it gives you events, accountabilities, and artifacts, and deliberately leaves the *how* to the team. Core narrative: the problem Scrum solves (complex, changing work where the path isn't knowable up front); what Scrum is *not* (a status-reporting ritual, a way to "do more faster," a substitute for engineering excellence). **Resource:** "Scrum on one page" PDF. **Assignment:** in 150 words, explain to a skeptical manager why Scrum ≠ status meetings (rubric: names empiricism, value delivery, self-management).

**L2 · Empiricism: Transparency, Inspection, Adaptation.** Empirical process control replaces up-front prediction with frequent inspect-and-adapt. Narrative: why transparency is the precondition (you can't inspect what you can't see); inspection cadence via the Scrum events; adaptation as the point of every event. **Resource:** empirical-pillars checklist. **Assignment:** map each Scrum event to the pillar it primarily serves.

**L3 · The Five Scrum Values.** Commitment, Focus, Openness, Respect, Courage — operationalized. Narrative: values as the behavioral substrate that makes the mechanics work; concrete examples of each value present vs absent. **Resource:** values self-assessment. **Assignment:** identify a past team conflict and which value would have changed the outcome.

**L4 · Scrum vs Waterfall (and where each fits).** Decision lens: complexity (Cynefin), cost of change curve, feedback latency. Narrative: Scrum is not always right — when predictive fits. **Resource:** "is it complex?" decision tree. **Assignment:** classify three real initiatives as complex/complicated/simple and justify.

**L5 · The Scrum Framework End-to-End.** Events, accountabilities, artifacts and how they interlock in one sprint. **Resource:** the Scrum flow diagram. **Assignment:** annotate the flow with the transparency each artifact provides.

**L6 · Sprints & the Heartbeat of Delivery.** Why a fixed cadence; sprint goal as the commitment; no scope creep mid-sprint vs adaptation between sprints. **Assignment:** write a sprint goal for a sample product increment.

**L7 · Your AI Co-Pilot for Scrum.** Setting up an AI mentor: prompting for explanations, generating facilitation agendas, drafting communications, and — critically — *critiquing* AI output. Narrative: AI accelerates, the human judges. **Resource:** starter prompt library. **Assignment:** use the co-pilot to draft a working agreement, then mark what you'd change and why.

**L8 · Common Anti-Patterns & How to Coach Them.** "Scrum-but," mechanical Scrum, the SM-as-secretary, velocity-as-target. **Assignment:** diagnose two anti-patterns from short vignettes and propose a coaching move.

**L9 · Module Synthesis & Readiness.** Tie pillars + values + framework into a coherent stance; preview the role (M2). **Assignment:** one-paragraph "why Scrum" you could deliver to an exec.

## Module Exam — 60 questions (blueprint + samples)
**Mix:** 18 easy / 30 medium / 12 hard. **Tag coverage:** Scrum (28), Agile (16), AI (10), Leadership (6). Every item: 4 options, 1 correct, rationale.

*Authored samples (representative of the 60):*
1. *(easy · Scrum)* Scrum is best described as a — A) methodology B) **framework** C) project plan D) maturity model. *Rationale: Scrum defines events/accountabilities/artifacts and leaves the how to the team.*
2. *(easy · Agile)* Empiricism rests on three pillars: transparency, inspection, and — A) estimation B) **adaptation** C) documentation D) governance.
3. *(med · Scrum)* A stakeholder demands a fixed scope and date for a complex product. The most Scrum-aligned response is to — A) commit to both B) **make progress transparent and adapt via sprints** C) extend the sprint D) skip the retro. *Rationale: empirical control over prediction.*
4. *(med · AI)* Your AI co-pilot drafts a retrospective agenda. The best next step is — A) run it verbatim B) **review/edit it against the team's context, then run** C) discard it D) forward it to the PO. *Rationale: AI accelerates; the human judges.*
5. *(hard · Leadership)* A team hits its velocity target but value delivered is falling. The SM should first — A) raise the velocity target B) **inspect outcomes vs output and coach toward the sprint goal** C) add scope D) report green. *Rationale: velocity is a capacity signal, not a goal.*
6. *(med · Agile)* Which best signals you should NOT use Scrum? A) changing requirements B) **a well-understood, repeatable task with no uncertainty** C) need for fast feedback D) cross-functional work.
